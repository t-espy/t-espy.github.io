Building software with AI for organizations that can't afford it to fail

I architect and build AI-assisted software delivery systems, and I bring that to enterprise and public-sector teams where reliability, security, and governance matter: contact-center platforms handling about 100,000 calls an hour, regulated healthcare and state government work, and now agent pipelines with independent verification. My approach is simple: pin down the problem, write down what done looks like, let AI agents do the heavy lifting inside clear limits, and never let the agent grade its own work.

Recent work includes Ratchetloop, where AI agents write code and independent checks review it; Strategy Assayer, which tests whether a result is real or luck; and Meetup Finder, a live app that finds a fair place for a group to meet.

Before this, I built production systems for enterprise and public-sector clients, including Verizon, Kaiser Permanente, Walmart, and state government. Ratchetloop - Auditable AI Software Delivery

In plain terms: AI agents write the code, independent checks and a second AI from a different model family review it, and a person decides what ships.

A local-first software-delivery pipeline for AI coding agents. A bounded task goes to a coder in an isolated Git worktree, deterministic checks validate the change, a different model family reviews the diff, and Ratchetloop leaves a branch and a full record of what happened, so a person can decide whether to merge it.

Strategy Assayer - Computational Research & Evaluation Platform

In plain terms: it separates results that are real from results that are luck, so decisions are not made on noise.

The largest system I have built on my own. It runs thousands of experiments in parallel, then puts the best ones through harder tests (held-out data, random controls, and corrections for trying many ideas at once) before any result is trusted. A 679,140-point search finished in under three minutes instead of an estimated 88 hours. The same approach fits any problem where a promising result has to hold up.

Meetup Finder - A Fair Place to Meet, No Sign-Up

In plain terms: a group enters where everyone is, and the app suggests a fair place to meet, with no sign-up.

The design starts from three privacy boundaries: no accounts, no app install, and no location tracking. It computes the meeting point itself rather than calling paid map services. Those three rules shaped everything else, and the app went from idea to a live beta.
