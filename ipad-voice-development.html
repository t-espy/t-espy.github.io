The Future of Development Is a Person Muttering at a Tablet in a Spare Bedroom

Voice in, screen out, and a Linux box doing the work

I keep seeing the same picture in my head: a developer on a couch, iPad in hand, earbuds in, quietly running a software project.

I'm about 70% sure the picture is right. The earbuds are wrong.

Talking beats typing, especially on glass

Typing on an iPad is the worst of both worlds: a full-size screen with a keyboard you can't feel. Published studies on phones put speech at roughly 1.4x to 3x faster than the on-screen keyboard, depending on whether you test in a lab or in real life.

Nobody has measured it on tablets, but the logic carries. The place where typing is slowest is the place where talking wins by the most. And holding a tablet while you talk at it feels less ridiculous than addressing a 27-inch monitor.

Output belongs on a screen

Here's where I part ways with the earbuds crowd. I don't want the answer read to me. Diffs, plans, and test output are things you scan, and scanning is a visual job. Speech is for input only. The only reason to wear earbuds is a quiet background, so the transcriber hears you and not the dishwasher.

The rig

A Linux box, tmux, and Claude Code, plus whatever dev tooling you'd need anyway. Turn on Remote Control, scan a QR code, and the iPad is a window into a session that lives on the box. No tunnel, no VPN, no port forwarding. The iPad needs nothing installed beyond the Claude app.

The box does the long-running work, holds the context, and runs the tools. The tablet is just where you talk and where you read.

The catch

You need a quiet room, so this will not conquer open-plan offices or coffee shops. The agent will also ask permission to run things, so you still have to answer it. The future of development, then, is not everyone wearing earbuds. It's one person in a spare bedroom, muttering at a tablet and approving a diff.

I'm okay with that future.
